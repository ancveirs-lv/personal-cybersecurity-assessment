# Personal Cybersecurity Self-Assessment

> This is practical self-assessment guidance, not a guarantee of safety, legal advice or a substitute for incident-response support.

## Response states

- `UNKNOWN` — Not sure
- `NOT_IN_PLACE` — Not in place
- `PARTIAL` — Partly / inconsistently
- `CONSISTENT` — Consistently in place
- `VERIFIED` — Checked or tested

## Assessment

### Accounts and authentication

#### P01

Important accounts use unique passwords or passkeys rather than reused passwords.

**Recommended action:** Start with primary email and banking; remove reused passwords.

**Sources:** `nist_sp800_63b`, `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P02

A password manager or equivalent safe method is used for passwords that must be stored.

**Recommended action:** Adopt protected password storage and secure its account.

**Sources:** `nist_sp800_63b`, `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P03

Multi-factor or phishing-resistant authentication is enabled on important accounts where available.

**Recommended action:** Enable passkeys or strong MFA on high-impact accounts first.

**Sources:** `nist_sp800_63b`, `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P04

Recovery channels, recovery codes and active sessions are current and under my control.

**Recommended action:** Review recovery channels and remove obsolete sessions and devices.

**Sources:** `nist_sp800_63b`

### Devices and software

#### P05

Phones and computers use a screen lock and are not left openly accessible.

**Recommended action:** Enable a suitable device lock and reasonable auto-lock delay.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P06

Operating systems, browsers and important apps receive security updates promptly.

**Recommended action:** Enable automatic updates where suitable and install outstanding security updates.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P07

Built-in security protections are enabled and I know if a device is no longer supported.

**Recommended action:** Check security settings and identify unsupported devices.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P08

Old, lost or sold devices are removed from important accounts and handled securely.

**Recommended action:** Review trusted-device lists and securely erase devices before transfer.

**Sources:** `nist_sp800_63b`

### Data and backups

#### P09

Important photos, documents and other data have a backup outside the primary device.

**Recommended action:** Back up the data you would most regret losing to a separate protected location.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P10

I have verified that at least one important backup can actually be restored.

**Recommended action:** Restore a sample file or backup and note what worked.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P11

Sensitive files and cloud storage are protected by appropriate account and sharing controls.

**Recommended action:** Review cloud sharing links, permissions and account protection.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P12

I avoid keeping passwords, recovery codes or sensitive secrets in openly readable notes or messages.

**Recommended action:** Move exposed secrets to protected storage and remove unnecessary copies.

**Sources:** `nist_sp800_63b`

### Fraud and verification

#### P13

Unexpected urgency, secrecy or emotional pressure makes me pause before acting.

**Recommended action:** Use a deliberate pause rule for requests involving money or credentials.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P14

For important actions I open the official service myself instead of trusting a message link.

**Recommended action:** Navigate independently to the known official service for banking and account changes.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P15

Unusual requests from known people or organisations are verified through another channel.

**Recommended action:** Use a separately known contact method before acting.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P16

I do not install remote-access software or share verification codes because an unsolicited caller or message tells me to.

**Recommended action:** Treat unsolicited remote-access or code-sharing requests as a stop condition.

**Sources:** `nist_sp800_63b`, `ncsc_top_tips`

### Networks, apps and sharing

#### P17

My home router/Wi-Fi uses current security settings and a non-default administrative credential.

**Recommended action:** Review router admin credentials, update status and wireless security mode.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P18

I avoid saving passwords or leaving sessions open on shared or public devices.

**Recommended action:** Do not save credentials and sign out fully on shared devices.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P19

I periodically review app permissions and remove access that is not needed.

**Recommended action:** Review location, contacts, microphone, camera and account permissions.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P20

I use protective DNS or equivalent network protection where appropriate and available.

**Recommended action:** Consider a reputable protective DNS or equivalent network protection appropriate to your environment.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

### Incident and recovery readiness

#### P21

I know the first actions to take if an important account is compromised.

**Recommended action:** Write a short recovery order: regain control, change or revoke credentials and review recovery settings.

**Sources:** `nist_sp800_63b`

#### P22

I know how to revoke old sessions, devices or credentials after suspected compromise.

**Recommended action:** Locate session/device management for your primary email before you need it.

**Sources:** `nist_sp800_63b`

#### P23

I know where to report cyber incidents or fraud and when financial loss also requires contacting my bank or police.

**Recommended action:** Save official reporting and bank contact paths before an incident.

**Sources:** `ncsc_top_tips`

#### P24

After a security incident I review what else may be affected instead of fixing only the visible symptom.

**Recommended action:** Check linked accounts, recovery channels, payment methods and affected devices.

**Sources:** `nist_sp800_63b`, `ncsc_top_tips`
